# The Ancient Art of War at Sea remake: plan

Drafted 12 Nov 2026 after the first analysis of the At Sea disk (`WAS_Analysis/README.md`, `WAS_Analysis/WAS_file_reference.md`). The decisions below are carried over from the WAR remake (`WAR_Remake`, `Remake_plan.md` in the project) unless marked **to agree**.

## Decisions carried over
- **Rewrite, not emulate.** C + SDL3, CMake, a macOS app bundle (also builds on Linux and Windows). Logic ported function by function from `WAS_Analysis/WAS.EXE.asm`; each C function names the routine it ports (e.g. `/* ovl4:85D2 */`), and keeps WAS.EXE's units and state so it can be checked against the asm and against the original in DOSBox.
- **Assets are read at run time from the player's own copy** (WAS.EXE, SM, SP*, B, AD, SO0-SO8, S0-SA, SITLES/SDITLES, SCNF, WAST.DAT). No original artwork, campaigns or fonts in the remake's code or repo; the app icon is drawn at build time from the player's files.
- **CGA and 16-colour modes** draw the same 320x200 screen, switched at any time with Tab; F2 changes the aspect (4:3 or square pixels). The 16-colour set is the Tandy 1000 one from WAST.DAT, in the standard 16 colours.
- **Hercules** (asked for 12 Nov, after step 3): Tab's third mode, or `HERC` on the command line as `WAS HERC`. The CGA screen is shown as WAS.EXE shows it on a Hercules card (ovl1:CE78): each 2-bit pixel as two mono pixels, 640x200 at 32,72 of a 720x348 screen (gfx.c screen_to_herc); the whole 720x348 is shown, lit pixels grey AAAAAAh as in DOSBox. Checked against DOSBox's machine=hercules.
- **Speed:** the IBM PC/XT is the reference; measure in DOSBox at 300 cycles, timings from video.
- **Data disks are folders** in `~/Library/Application Support/AncientArtOfWarAtSea/Remake/` (SDL_GetPrefPath("AncientArtOfWarAtSea", "Remake"), as WAR's AncientArtOfWar/Remake), keeping the original formats (S files, SITLES, SS, SCNF) so the original can read what the remake writes; original saved games imported.
- **Deliberate differences** are listed here and agreed with the user first.
- **Source files** start with the user's header (agreed 12 Nov), UTF-8 with a BOM:
  ```
  /*
   * The Ancient Art of War at Sea (1987) - remake
   * Nov 2026 using Claude Opus 5.5
   * "Guided" by Brett Hallen
   * Original game by Dave Murry & Barry Murry
   * Published by Brøderbund Software Inc.
   *
   * file.c: what it does
   */
  ```
- **App name:** AncientArtOfWarAtSea (agreed 12 Nov): the CMake project and executable, and the Application Support folder (above).
- **Build order:** the same as WAR's (agreed 12 Nov), below.

- **Own copies, no shared library** (agreed 12 Nov): `WAS_Remake/` has its own `src/`, `test/` and `tools/`, laid out like `WAR_Remake/`; the parts that carry over from WAR (gfx.c, icon.c, tools/make_icon.c, test/build.sh, later sound.c and the main loop) are copied and changed as At Sea needs. `WAR_Remake/` is left as it is.

- **Game log (asked 13 Nov, as the WAR remake's):** `--log` (normal) or `--log=verbose` / `--log verbose` on the command line. gamelog.c/h: each campaign started (main.c begin_game, the viewer's Enter too) or saved game resumed opens a new file in the remake's folder under Logs/, named "YYYY-MM-DD HH-MM-SS <title>.log" (local time); the demo isn't logged (begin_demo closes any open log). Header: when, how, title and S file, opponent and its 25 values with their uses, random()'s state, the squadrons, the Options Shoppe (fight delay, wind, reload, aim), the 9 rules in the Rulebook's words with their values and uses, a map of the cells; then the game at the start (changed opponent values, ports and crowns with their supplies, every squadron with its ships, strength and doing, the enemy's standing target, losses; verbose: the strength and port-value maps). Lines carry [mm:ss tick N] (map iterations at 27 Hz, game ticks): ENEMY (enemy.c: supplies and repairs, guarding its crown, the standing target and dropping it, routes with their cells, cost and directions, attacks with the strengths and values, arriving, joining, merchants unloading; play.c: the lost merchant's news, end_check's changes to its values, the offer), YOU (orders and answers), EVENT (sightings once per squadron, encounters, fights on the map with each hit and loss, merchants, ports, the wind, ships lost at sea, flagships made, crowns, the end), BATTLE with the battle's clock (setup, every ovl6_message, your orders, hits, boarding: Zoom/Pass, the crews, your commands, each man falling, the winner; the losses). Verbose adds the squadron looked at each turn and why it stays, each cell's score in choose_target, route searches, the maps when worked out again, supplies and repairs in port, broadsides, and a squadrons table every 16 ticks. A game's end writes the reason (DS:649F) and the state; Alt+S keeps the log open. Logging only reads the game: no random(), and none of the routines that count work for the XT's timing (gamelog.c has its own pure copies of squadron_cell, strength, the tile classes); test_gamelog.c plays every campaign twice side by side with the same keys, one logged and one not, and compares the Play structs and the battles byte for byte after every iteration and battle slice (normal and verbose; 131 battles, 20 boardings); test_app_log.c checks main.c's handling.

## Deliberate differences
- Step 7 (14 Nov, to agree): no disk prompts in the editors ("Put DATA disk in drive B" and the WAS disk's; a new data disk's SITLES comes from SDITLES without "Shall I make a new data disk?"); Esc backs out of "Select the campaign you want to change." to the campaign menu (WAS.EXE has no way back there); changing or removing on a data disk with no campaigns says "No campaigns on this data disk." for 2 s and goes back to the campaign menu (WAS.EXE: "Not found, <R>etry or <Q>uit"); "THIS CAMPAIGN IS COMPLETE" stays 2 1/2 s before the title page (WAS.EXE goes on at once, so it is hardly seen); a file that can't be written says "Unable to use data disk. Press any key to continue."; a data disk's S files are in its folder, SITLES 876 bytes as the original writes it. (As in WAR.)
- Tab switches CGA / 16 colours (in WAS.EXE Tab scrolls up, as - does); F2 changes the aspect.
- Esc in play asks "Quit to the title page? (Y or N)" (no Esc in WAS.EXE). After saving, Q returns to the title page (WAS.EXE quits to DOS). (As in WAR.)
- No copy protection, no disk prompts; a data disk is a folder (Data Disks/<name>/ with SITLES, the S files, SS), chosen in the Options Shoppe (the "data disk uses a:/b:" line becomes "disk <name>" / "a new data disk") or, with more than one, from a list that ( data disk ) shows. A new data disk gets SITLES from SDITLES. (As in WAR.)
- "presented by" stays for the tune's length (6.75 s) or 3 s without music; a key skips it. (As in WAR.)
- The end of a demo game stays 3 s (WAS.EXE blinks "You Won" / "You Lost" until a key). (As in WAR.)
- Options Shoppe: display "black/white" turns the whole picture grey (on a plain CGA the setting does nothing); "center the screen" is skipped by the pointer; the joystick setting is stored but not used. (As in WAR.)
- Tunes and beeps are counted, not heard, until step 8; the time a tune took is kept (the report's "Press any key" comes after it, the opening's lines with it).
- Info, Fleet and Pause are screens of the main loop rather than loops of their own (WAS.EXE waits in them); time is stopped in them as in the original.
- Keys other than the marker's act once per press (WAS.EXE reads a held key again every iteration, so a held T steps the time on more than once).
- Detach / Join, the opponent's offer and "You Won" / "You Lost" are screens of the main loop too; WAS.EXE's delays (after a message, a command) skip iterations rather than stop the program, and a key pressed meanwhile waits, as in the keyboard buffer.
- (Since step 6 the opponent's offer at the start of a game is shown over the quote's page, as in WAS.EXE.)
- (The viewer itself is the remake's own, as in WAR.)
