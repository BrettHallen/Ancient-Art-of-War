# The Ancient Art of War at Sea: analysis

Analysis of the files on the At Sea 3.5" disk (`WAS_Original_Files_3.5`), started 12 Nov 2026 after the WAR remake was finished, following the same method (see `Handoff_At_Sea.md`).

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `WAS_file_reference.md` | Every file on the disk: what it holds and how it is laid out |
| `WAS.EXE.asm` | Annotated disassembly of WAS.EXE (the CGA version), 61,000 lines |
| `disassembler/wasdis.py`, `disassembler/gen_asm.py` | The disassembler that writes `WAS.EXE.asm` (`python3 gen_asm.py`, needs capstone). ## Findings so far

### At Sea is built like WAR
- WAS.EXE is linked with Phoenix PLINK86 and uses the same overlay loader as WAR.EXE; the same compiler and much of the same C code. 105 of WAR.EXE's named routines are found unchanged in WAS.EXE (matched by instruction sequence): the C library, file I/O, text output, the 8x8 font (byte-identical), sound, delay and random(), the scroll and menu helpers, the hardware detection and key-disk check, the editors' key and cursor handling, some campaign-play routines (select_squad, show_time_setting, msg_encounter, print_map_region, play_end_sound ...). Where a routine is the same, the WAR analysis and the WAR remake's port of it apply.
- The overlays map onto WAR's: overlay 1 = WAR's overlay 1 (main program), 2 = WAR's 3 (squads editor, now ships), 3 = WAR's 4 (map maker), 4 = WAR's 6 (campaign play). The battle is new: overlay 5 (sea battle, with overlay 6 beside it) and overlay 7 (boarding).
- The resident code is much bigger (6C50h bytes against 28D0h) and there are 61 overlay thunks (WAR: 21).
- The units look the same as WAR's: a 10 x 20 tile map of 32x20 tiles (320x400 pixels), ship x in byte columns and y in line pairs, 9999 for an empty slot, -1/0 for the two sides, 2 bits per pixel in an off-screen buffer with odd lines at +3E80h.

### What is different
- **New picture format** (load_picture ovl1:CBEA): word count per field, codes <= 200 are runs, > 200 literals. All 14 CGA pictures decode exactly to 2 x 8000 bytes.
- **WAST.DAT has no palettes**: the 16-colour pictures use the standard 16 colours. The 16-colour version is for the Tandy 1000 (WAST.EXE); it has 12 pictures (no 16-colour SPP or SPQ battle reports).
- **94 map tiles** (WAR: 72), with place names, ports, crowns, Neptune and sea serpents.
- **Campaigns S0-SA** (2180 or 2220 bytes): story, map, 40 squadrons (side, speed, position, supplies) and 40 ships (squadron, sails damage, type, name, hull damage; at most 3 ships to a squadron), opponent, 9 rule words. WAR had 20 squads and 10 flags. Saved game **SS** (10,444 bytes of DS).
- **Map sprites** in the data segment: anchor, crossed swords (an encounter), a ship in 16 headings, the flagship's pennant and the merchant's cargo. They are drawn as WAR's squads were (white with a black outline for the player, the reverse for the enemy), but the outline stays within each byte.
- **6 opponents** (Thor Foote, Medina Sidonia, Blackbeard, Tromp, John Paul Jones, Nelson).
- **No manual word question**; only the key-disk check.
- CGA palette 1 (bright cyan / magenta / white) for everything but the scrolls, which are in palette 0 as in WAR (checked in DOSBox).
- **Hercules** (`WAS HERC`: rt_0000 sets [686D] when the first argument starts with H or h; detect_video ovl1:CE0C also falls back to it when no CGA answers): ovl1_CE78 programs the card for 720x348 graphics (CRTC table DS:0D2F, mode 8Ah: page 1 at B800h, the same segment as CGA) and fills the row table DS:0D3B so that CGA row n is Hercules scanline 72 + n, from byte 4 (four banks of 2000h, 90 bytes a row). Everything else draws exactly as for CGA, so each 2-bit CGA pixel shows as two mono pixels (0 dark-dark, 1 dark-lit, 2 lit-dark, 3 lit-lit): a 640x200 picture in the middle of the 720x348 screen. [0ECB] = 1 then: the palette is not changed (set_cga_palette_if rt:30AE) and the Options Shoppe skips one of its lines. Checked in DOSBox (machine=hercules).

### Campaign play (overlay 4), as far as the map goes
- campaign_play (ovl4:7650) has WAR's shape (WAR.EXE ovl6:3197): load SM and SO5 (the messages, to DS:4AF5), then the loop: blit the map, every [6312] iterations a tick (time slow 64, medium 16, fast 4, faster 1; no ticks while a key is down) with encounters (ovl4:8BCD), move_squadrons (ovl4:96A0), eat (ovl4:9345) for two squadrons, every 10, 20, 25 and 31 ticks more work (below), then campaign_input.
- The view: DS:3F3B is the top line pair (0-109), 182 lines of map with two text rows; the marker DS:5CC7 (byte 1-78) and DS:5CC9 (line pair 0-195), its buffer address DS:5CCB; the keypad's steps at DS:0739/0744. The marker stays within 86 line pairs of the top; - and + scroll by 2 line pairs; Shift jumps to the top (marker 38,45) or bottom (38,145).
- The marker is over a squadron when it is in the squadron's byte column and 2 line pairs above to 2 below the squadron's address line (y+1). The command line then shows Move (yours, not in an encounter), Info, Detach (2 or more ships and a free slot), Join (a squadron of up to two ships within 3 bytes and 7 line pairs of another of its side; the remake's step 2 had "one-ship", fixed in step 3), Zoom (in an encounter), Practice.
- Per squadron at run time: DS:56FF in an encounter (drawn as crossed swords: the sprite at DS:04AD is not a wreck), 574F seen, 583F on a traced path (path memory: the story's buffer DS:4E53, 1500 entries), 588F on a route (route memory DS:542F, 600 bytes), 5EE5 control (-2 at the start; commandable = 5EE5 -1, or side -1 and 5EE5 != 0), 5EBD pennant / merchant, 5FD5 heading, 6581 speed before putting into port.
- Info (squadron_info rt:074B) draws a panel over the map (lines 62-136): each ship's name, type and sails and hull bars, the supplies bar, a 0-100 scale, "SPEED: Anchored / Half Sail / Full Sail" (S changes it only for a squadron on a traced path), "Encounter" or "In Port" over the speed.
- Fleet (ovl4:AD63) counts the flagships, ships of the line and frigates on each side (counted by who commands the squadron).
- **Speed:** at 300 cycles (about an IBM PC/XT) the marker moves 27 bytes a second with a key held (video at 30 fps): the main loop runs about 27 times a second when nothing else is happening. WAS.EXE adds a delay only on faster PCs ([0D19] > 514h).
- Checked in DOSBox: the start, the command line over a squadron, Info, Fleet and the Surrender question are pixel-identical in the remake.

### Campaign play: the rules (overlay 4; the remake's step 3)
All of this is ported in `WAS_Remake/src/play.c` and was checked against the original in DOSBox (`WAS_Remake/test/test_rules.c` lists the scenes).
- **The clock** (campaign_play ovl4:7959): every [6312] iterations, when no key is down and the player is not tracing, a tick: encounters (8BCD), the enemy (4 x C79A, below), move_squadrons (96A0), eat (9345) for the next two squadrons. Tick 10: a 75/128 chance of restock_ports (BCD2); tick 20: crowns_check (C51A); tick 25: with the wind on (SCNF [6865]) a new wind with chance 16 or 64 /128 (rule [64BB] 1 or 2); after tick 30 the messages' timer [630D] counts down and the count starts again. Every iteration (not in the prompt mode 99): map_battles (8EDE).
- **Paths** are a ring of 1500 signed address steps at DS:4E53 (-80 up a line pair, 1 a byte right, 79/81 diagonals; at most 80 a step, so a scroll of two line pairs is two steps), 0 free, 99 the end. M (start_move_order 89E4) needs a free run of 15 after the writing position [62F7] (find_free_path_run ECD1). While tracing ([65AA] = 2) time stands still, the marker is a box (draw_cursor rt:280E: the even lines of five line pairs below it, 8 pixels wide) that moves on every other iteration ([64D1]) and not onto land: trace_blocked (B3E5) looks at the tiles under it (tile_is_land), then at the map buffer's bytes (44h and 11h are land) and the slant of the cell's shore. Running into another path ends the tracing. M again (end_trace 8A75) writes 99, sets speed 2 (full sail) and the destination [6316] (a little cross, rt:2AB8); C (cancel_path 8A2C) forgets it.
- **Sailing** (move_squadrons 96A0): a squadron's countdown [56AF] counts the ticks; at 0 it takes the next step and the countdown is set to [64E1] (set_move_delays 9AB2): 3 - speed, doubled if any ship is not a frigate, +1 if a ship's sails are damaged more than 2, +2 more than 4 (one each at most), and shifted left by DS:0ECD (1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 1, 1) when heading within three points of the wind's direction; merchants always 3. The heading is the average of the next two steps' (step_heading 9A3C, a sparse switch: -80 N=0, -79 3, 1 4, 81 5, 80 8, 79 11, -1 12, -81 13). The marker follows the selected squadron. At 99 the squadron anchors; yours call port_call (BD86), and crown_check (C44E) runs.
- **Rough and shallow water** (in set_move_delays): a frigate on a rough tile (10-12, 16-18) and a ship of the line or flagship on a shallow tile (7-9, 13-15) are lost with chance 2/128, or about twice that when the rule (64B3 rough, 64B9 shallow) is 0; 1 is safe. The enemy's only one time in four. lost_at_sea (9D7E): the message (name, or ENEMY FRIGATE / ENEMY SHIP, SUNK IN ROUGH WATERS / LOST IN SHALLOW WATER), the squadron's first other ship of the line becomes a flagship (whatever was lost: promote_at_sea C302), and the loss is counted by [5EE5] (so, outside a fight, as the enemy's: count_loss 92B7 with way 0).
- **Supplies** (eat 9345, two squadrons a tick): -2 with chance (rule 64B5 x 32 - 10)/128 for yours, opponent [6059]/128 for the enemy's; below 4, a 20/128 chance of damage to each ship. An anchored squadron with no enemy in its cell takes +5 from the supply port (75) beside it with the most left, or a crown (76, 77), and with chance (rule 64AD)/128 a repair (one less damage to sails and hull of each ship) from the repair port (74) or a crown. Ports hold rule 64AF (repair) or twice that (supply ports, crowns) at the start (port_supplies C197); each use takes one unless 64AF >= 20; an empty port is drawn inverted (invert_tile_buf rt:2E61). "Beside" is one of the four neighbours whose shore faces the squadron's cell all along (coast_blocks rt:1C0C, table DS:0796, shore types DS:425D from coast_init rt:2523).
- **Ports refill** (port_call BD86) when they run low: at once if beside a crown or if no merchant can come (no sea way from the port's cell to the edge of the map: sea_way E5BF, a breadth-first search on the work arrays came (3F3D) and queue (457D, which runs on into the strength map past 100 cells), the queue written as queue[l6++] (the remake had read it as queue[++l6] until step 6, so it found only ports beside the edge); port_sources C07C at the start); otherwise a merchant squadron ([5EBD] = 2, cargo sprite) is created at the edge and given a route (BEE3, with the enemy's route finder: not yet read). restock_ports (BCD2) calls port_call for ports with 2 or less.
- **Encounters and sighting** (encounters 8BCD): your squadrons and all merchants against the enemy's and all merchants. Close (|dx| < 3 bytes, |dy| < 5 line pairs: close_to C5B9): merchants of the same side resupply a warship (meet_merchant BB29: 20 supplies, MERCHANT SUPPLIES SQUAD; the merchant is used up if the warship had under 10), a merchant is taken by the other side (take_merchant BACD: +10, MERCHANT LOST / ENEMY MERCHANT CAPTURED), two warships meet: crossed swords ([56FF] = partner + 1), [5EE5] = 1, stopped (their speed kept in 6581), WARNING - ENCOUNTER. Otherwise sighted when 2|dx| + |dy| < visibility (rule 64B7; with 80 everything is seen and the message comes under 20): ALERT - ENEMY SIGHTED, the first time.
- **Map battles** (map_battles 8EDE, every iteration): an encounter's [5EE5] counts up to the fight delay [6851] (Options Shoppe: 250, 500, 1000) by 1, +1 with more than 5 enemy squadrons, more on fast clocks with more than 10 and 15; then [5EE5] = the side (A L E R T - FIGHTING for yours) and each iteration the squadron's side flips (the crossed swords flash) and with chance 40/128 its partner may be hit (battle_hit 9154: chance 65/128 for yours, opponent [6053] for the enemy's, -10 beside a crown (WAS.EXE passes the squadron's number as the cell); a ship of the line escapes 64/128, a flagship 90/128; the hull is hit, or the sails with chance 62/128 while they hold; hull 5 sinks it: URGENT - FLAG TAKEN / ENEMY FLAG CAPTURED for a flagship). When a squadron has no ships left: NOTICE - BATTLE WON / LOST, and end_check.
- **The end** (end_check 9E4F after a loss): a crown taken by the survivor; no ships left (codes 12/2, 14/4 after a sea battle [630F]); no flagships left (flags_check C3C9, code 10/0), unless the side began without a flagship but has its crown on the map ([65BA], [65BB]; Race for the Crown); otherwise, if you have more than 6 squadrons and at least [605B] times theirs, the opponent offers to surrender (box over rows 3-6; Y ends the game, code 1, except when the offer came from a merchant meeting). crown_check (C44E): a squadron beside the other side's crown (76 the enemy's, 77 yours) with no enemy squadron in its cell takes it: WHITE/BLACK CROWN CAPTURED, code 3 (won) or 13. game_over (A129): You Won / You Lost blinks (7000 delay units each) until a key; after crowns_check on tick 20 that key ends the game at once, otherwise Enter does. end_check also sets the opponent's values [6057], [6065], [6067], [6071], [6073] for the enemy (step 5).
- **Detach / Join** (detach_join A347): the supplies are shared out by ships; a panel of two columns of names, + / - mark a ship (only ships that can move), S switches it, E ends; a squadron made by Detach goes one byte east or west, or two line pairs north or south, where there is sea (place_detached rt:1F2D).
- **The wind** (wind_change C658): 0-3 = from N, E, S, W; the face over tile 73 shows it (tiles 94, 92, 91, 93 = DS:67A8, 6660, 65BC, 6704); "Wind is from the ...".
- **Messages**: message_row (BBBC) adds up to two rows above the command line, then scrolls; print_map_region (BC2D) adds " IN THE NORTH/SOUTH WEST/EAST" or "INTERIOR" and waits 5000 delay units (0.25 s on an XT). [6890] keeps a sighting or flag message from repeating.
- **game_init** (ovl1:77AD) for a new game: [5EE5] = -2, the opponent's [6073] at most half the enemy's squadrons, a side with no flagship and no crown has a ship promoted (promote_flagship ovl1:7A3F), the wind at random, counts of supply ports (6499), repair ports (649B) and enemy crowns (649D). campaign_play then drops squadrons that start on land (the tile, or land bytes under it).
- **random()** (rt:2F0D) adds successive words of the resident code (from offset BB8h) to its last value, 0-127, seeded from the BIOS clock (seed_random rt:2EF9); delay() also calls it, so the sequence depends on timing.

### The enemy commander (overlay 4; the remake's step 5)
Ported in `WAS_Remake/src/enemy.c`. Checked routine by routine against WAS.EXE's own code under an emulator (`WAS_Remake/test/diff_ovl4`: the remake's state written out in WAS.EXE's data-segment layout by `dsmap.c`, the routine run in both, the data segments compared byte by byte): the enemy's orders, the strength map, the port values, encounters, sailing, eating, map battles and restocking matched in every sample, about 180 each over the 11 campaigns.
- **Orders** (enemy_orders C79A, four times a tick unless you are tracing a path): the next squadron after [64DD] that is anchored, not in an encounter and either the enemy's or a merchant of either side ([62FF] while it is given orders). One on a route ([588F]) follows it (follow_route CFEF); the others get new orders (new_orders C88A).
- **New orders** (C88A): beside a port or crown ([47D5] not 0) a squadron simply stays when random() is over [6057]/8 (and no standing target is set, [6898]); unless [6057] is 115 it also stays to take on supplies (under 18) or repairs where the port can serve (port_ok E554: more than 1 left, or the last port of its kind), or near one of your squadrons; beside its own crown it stays while no more than [6073] of its side are there. Otherwise choose_target (CAEA): out of a cell that is all land; to a supply port when supplies are under [605D], a repair port when the worst damage is over [605F] (port_search E103: the nearest cell beside one that can serve, or a crown, by cost); a crown target with [604D] or more squadrons bound for it ([5F35], bound_for EEBF) is dropped with chance 60/128. Then the whole map: a cell beside your crown (value 160 in the port-value map) is taken at once while fewer than [6073] are bound for it; your crown's side (250) or a cell with your flagship (-7 in the strength map) become the standing target [6309] with chance [6075]/128 once neither side has ports; otherwise the best of the cells your squadrons are in, weighed by their strength against the squadron's own and [6071], half the port value at times, less a random amount with chance [6061]/128. The route to the target sets out the other way round ([69CB]) with chance 64/128.
- **Routes** (find_route DB21): a cheapest-cost search over the 200 cells from the squadron to [64DF]: each cell costs its danger ([6071] - strength) / 2 + 3 where your strength is near, plus 120 (or the rule x 32) for rough water with a frigate aboard and shallow water with a ship of the line, unless the rule says it is safe; directions are tried toward the target first, in the order at SO5+12h; the queue (DS:457D, 100 cells) runs on into the next arrays when it overflows, as in WAS.EXE. The route, a direction a cell, goes into the ring DS:542F (600 bytes; at most 21 steps), and only its first half is kept: the squadron sails half way and then looks again. A leg is sailed as a path in the path ring (sail_to D99E: at most 30 steps to the next cell's middle, at full sail).
- **Following a route** (CFEF): first to the middle of its cell; then a warship not bound for a crown looks for a victim (pick_victim D5C2): the nearest of your squadrons or merchants (or its own side's merchants when its supplies are under [605D] + 5), not stronger than its own strength + [6065] unless the route has ended or the victim has a flagship (a squadron with a flagship holds back with chance [6055]/128), with no land or bad water on the way (way_barred D799: 5 cells at most), not in an encounter unless (second pass) at most two of its side are there; merchants count at half the distance. Within [6063] x 8 steps, and not hungry or damaged with ports to go to, it sails at it (up to 8 steps). Otherwise the next leg; at the end of the route it looks for a new one to the same destination, or has arrived: a merchant fills the supply port beside (twice rule [64AF]) and the repair port (rule [64AF]) and is gone; a warship moves a little, joins others close by (enemy_join ED83: a squadron of fewer than three ships takes the ships of its partner), and calls on the port.
- **The maps**: the strength map DS:4645 (strength_map E805: every 31 ticks and after battles; 6 a frigate, 12 a ship of the line, 18 a flagship, less half the damage, yours negative, -7 where your flagship is) and the port values DS:47D5 (port_value_map EA51: the cells beside repair and supply ports by rule values, beside your crown 160, beside theirs 250). port_side (BD3C) uses the strength map to decide whose merchant restocks a port.
- **The opponent** (DS:604D-607D, from SO2): [604D] how many squadrons make a crown target crowded, [6055] a flagship squadron's caution, [6057] how readily it stays in port (115: never for supplies or repairs), [605D] hunger, [605F] damage it puts up with, [6061] randomness in choosing targets, [6063] how far it goes to attack (x 8 steps), [6065] the strength margin it attacks with, [6071] how much your strength puts it off, [6073] its crown's guard, [6075] the chance of going for your crown or flagship; [6077], [6079], [607B] in boarding.
- **Timing**: this work is what makes a tick slow on an XT. Under the emulator an enemy's orders take a few thousand instructions, a route search up to 400,000 (1.3 s at 300 a millisecond); encounters about 15,000, sailing 9,000 plus 3,350 + 500 a squadron shown to erase and draw the squadrons again. In DOSBox at 300 cycles The Bane of Oldain left alone ends with your crown taken after 127 s at the fastest time and 157 s at fast (about 2 ticks a second at the fastest, not 27). WAS.EXE keeps the game answering during this work (campaign_play ovl4:7924-7A83): a key read by poll_input ([6896]) skips the tick altogether, and key_waiting (ovl4:85F8: a key held down, [802], or one in the BIOS's buffer, [804]) is asked after the encounters, after each of the enemy's four orders, after move_squadrons and after eating; if a key is waiting the rest of the tick is left undone and campaign_input runs at once. So while you scroll with a key held the enemy does nothing, and a key pressed during the enemy's work waits only for the order being worked out. Seen in DOSBox at 300 cycles (13 Nov), The Bane of Oldain at the slow time, the marker key held 6 s up then 6 s down: the map stood still 10% of the time, 9 times for more than 0.5 s in 100 s (the longest 1.3 s), each time just as one key was let go and the other pressed. 129 s to the crown at the fastest time again (from the map's first appearance).

### The sea battle and boarding (overlays 5, 6 and 7; the remake's step 4)
All of this is ported in `WAS_Remake/src/battle.c`, `battle_ships.c`, `battle_gfx.c` and `boarding.c`, and was checked against the original in DOSBox (`WAS_Remake/test/test_battle.c` lists the scenes).
- **The way in**: Z over your squadron while an encounter counts down ([5EE5] > 0) or P over a lone commandable one ([5EE5] = -2: Practice, [6C8D] = 1, the guns load at their fastest). The shore [686B] comes from the map tile (types 1-8), a port beside (9-12) or the land around. campaign_play returns 2, rt_0000 calls ovl5_7650 and campaign_play is entered again afterwards; end_check runs unless you Left ([65B8]), and the zoomed flag [630F] changes its end codes.
- **Memory**: SO6 is read to DS:4AF5 (the battle's strings and tables), SO7 to DS:4257, SO8 (4,83Ch bytes: the ships' close-view pictures, the gun crews) into an allocated block whose offsets the pointer tables 6BA9-6C97 hold; the gun crews' panel is built at DS:3F3D (44 bytes x 18 lines). The screen is B800h written directly through the row table DS:0D3B; the view is rows 34-181, sprites in line pairs.
- **Ships**: six slots, 0-2 your squadron ([6303]), 3-5 the enemy's ([6301]); x 69E7 (FC19h = no ship), y 69F3 in world units (the overview [69DD] = 2 draws at x/6 bytes and y/6 line pairs, the close view 3 at x - [69D5], y - [69D7]); owner 69FF (-1 yours, 0 the enemy's; a boarding changes it); type 69E1; sails 6A0B and hull 6A11 damage (hull > 5 strikes the colours, > 7 sinks); heading 6ACB (0-15) with 6AD7 the turn pending and 6AE3 the half step; orders 6A17 (1 Course, 2 mimic, 3 follow the ship in front, 4 general chase, 5 avoid battle, 6 board); the gun crew's side 6AA7 and the guns' loading 6AB3 / 6ABF (13 loaded); grappled 6B13; the drawing order 6B0D (by y, rt_5290). Several routines read index -1 or 6 of these arrays, so the neighbouring arrays' bytes matter (the remake rebuilds them).
- **The main loop** (ovl5_7650): every iteration the crew animation, the keys (ovl5_7ac6), the shot and splash particles (6B19.., 18 of them); every [10AA + time] iterations the ships' orders and aim (rt_34bf), the enemy's broadsides (rt_3eaa, chance 40/128), the end check (rt_51cf, then [6CD9] 40 or 10 iterations to the end), sailing (ovl5_b4c5 close / b841 overview, collisions ovl5_bc12) and the speeds every eighth time. Time 0-4 (faster, fast, medium, slow, slower; T steps it down and round).
- **Timing** at 300 cycles: Practice at the fastest time with one ship, 83 ms an iteration in the overview, 160-167 ms close.
- **Grappling** (ovl5_bc12): ships of the two sides touching may grapple; "X grapples Y. Zoom Pass" and a falling tone; Z boards (ovl7_c9b0), P passes. SO8 is freed for the boarding and loaded again after it.
- **Boarding** (overlay 7): a 7D00h-byte buffer ([3F37]) holds B as read (4,234 bytes) and, at +1F40h ([3F39]) and +5DC0h, the two fields of the deck (8,000 bytes each, the even and odd screen lines), unpacked from B again every frame (ovl7_fe6b, load_picture's format) before the men are drawn into it (ovl7_ff14: a picture of w bytes by h lines with a mask/value pair per byte for each field; your men AND NOT then OR, the enemy's OR then AND NOT) and the lines 17-75 copied to screen rows 34-152 (ovl7_fec9). The men's arrays follow B in the buffer: 6CF4 kind (1 officer), 6CF6 side (-1 / 0), 6CF8 place in the buffer, 6CFA x byte (0 gone), 6CFC y line, 6CFE facing (101 right, 102 left, 103, 104), 6D00 state (from the script: 1 clashing, 2 striking, 5 crossing, 9 dead), 6D02 part of the deck (1 flag, 2 quarterdeck, 3 poopdeck, 4 railing, 5 across; an officer's: his shot's frame), 6D04 opponent (an officer's: his shot's place), 6D06 drawing order, 6D08 on his way. DS:6D0A holds each man's place in his script: entries of three words (step in the buffer, picture number into the 48 pointers at 6D5A, state), ended by 101 or 102 (the facing); the scripts are at DS:1905-1CCD, the pictures at DS:1CD5-3E63, the parts' boxes at DS:193B (yours) and DS:1963 (the enemy's). 6DC7: 11 counts of men by part (+5 for the enemy's).
- **The crews** (boarding_load_b): yours 20 - 2 x your hull damage + 2 x theirs, -5 in a frigate, +5 against one, +5 when a ship is grappled to yours, -5 when one is grappled to theirs (so the ship grappled has the larger crew), kept within 15-25; the rest of the 40 are the enemy's. Each man is an officer with chance 27/128 (yours) or above the opponent's [6077]; officers stand anywhere free on the quarterdeck or poopdeck, the others in a part picked at random.
- **The fight** (ovl7_c9b0 loop, about 0.5 s a frame on an XT while the crews wait, 0.6 s as the fight starts): [64DD] = 20 until Attack (A sets 3; L leaves and returns -1); the opponent 2 attacks first with chance 20/128 a frame, opponent 4 with 1/128. Each frame (def1) every man looks for an opponent within 5 bytes and 2 lines (f3a9; else the nearest he may fight, f484), strikes (a hit with chance 20/128 for yours, the opponent's [607B]/128 for his, always against an officer, then 45/128), walks to his part of the deck (ec31 / ee50, the boxes) or to his opponent (f6e5), falls overboard or lies where he dies. Officers wave, stand or fire their pistols (chance 20/128, the opponent's [6079]). Every sixth frame the enemy gives orders (dbac). Your orders: FROM Flag / Quarterdeck / Poopdeck / Railing, TO the same or Cross (the enemy's flag); Again sends another; each order sends one man (d9f0). A side with no men left, or a man of the other side at its flag, loses ([6DC1] 1 you won, 2 you lost); 21 more frames of cheering and the loser's flag struck, then the loser's ship turns to lie alongside the winner's and changes owner.

### The editors (overlays 1, 2 and 3; the remake's step 7)
- **The flow** (rt_0270): *Make a new campaign.* runs change_campaign (ovl1:92C9: blank_armies, default_rules, the data disk's SITLES read in [6485] 9 without unrolling, [64DD] + 1 the new title, "Can't add any more campaigns!" past 11), the map maker (overlay 3, [6485] 3), the ships editor (overlay 2, [6485] 9) and new_campaign (ovl1:8395: choose_opponent [6485] 9, the story emptied, the rules [6485] 11, the story [6485] 0, write_campaign_file, the title [6485] 3, then "THIS CAMPAIGN IS COMPLETE" at 6,12); any step's Esc (5) ends at the title page. *Change a campaign.* (rt:037F): the titles with [6485] 7 (from title 1), then the change menu (editor_menu ovl1:843C: SPH and SO4's names at 1EFh, frames 1BFh/1CBh/1D7h, the same campaign_menu_choose A5F5): RULEBOOK (show_scroll 6: rules, save), STORYBOOK (10: the scroll editor, "Save the changes? (Y or N)"), MAP MAKER (overlay 3, [6485] 0), EXIT, ENEMY (choose_opponent 10, then save), FLEET (overlay 2, [6485] 0); the editors return 100 or 101 to save_or_reload (thunk 18: write_campaign_file or load_campaign again). *Remove* (8579): the titles with [6485] 5 ("Erase this title? (Y or N)"; Esc sets [6487] -1), then the file name moves to the end of the twelve at DS:0BB5, the titles below move up 64 bytes in the text, scroll_erase_line rubs the line out (33 cells, a blit and delay(500) each), the scroll rolls up and write_titles; the S file stays on the disk.
- **Files**: write_campaign_file (C527) writes the story (DS:4E53), the map, 1E0h bytes from DS:5CDD, the opponent, the 9 rules, the names (258h from DS:609F) and the hulls (DS:6025): 2180 bytes, as the WAS disk's shorter files. write_titles (C8D2) writes the 36 bytes of file names and the 840 of the titles' text (876; the WAS disk's SITLES is padded to 1024). A new data disk's SITLES is SDITLES (make_data_disk BF06 formats a floppy first).
- **The scroll editor** (B21E): reads DOS's keyboard (rt_313c puts the BIOS's int 9 back; rt_2e8e: int 21h 0Bh / 08h, an extended key read as 27 then its scan code, so Esc alone is 27 then 0); the pen (SO3 7D0h) at byte column + 7, line row x 12 + 58. In the editing modes (0, 3, 10) scroll_char keeps the text's marks: a capital puts 01h in the next cell (and clears a 01h after that), a letter over a capital clears its 01h. Story: arrows move, Enter goes to the next row, Backspace rubs out, Esc ends. Title (title_step 9200: the pen goes down to row 3 + 2 x [64DD]): columns 2-29 only, no space first, Enter ends it only with letters typed. Characters with no letter (\ > < ~ @ # $ % ^ & * = + `) are ignored.
- **The map maker** (ovl3_7650): SO0 and SM into BUF; four map rows at the top (the cursor's tile XORed), a 10 x 20 plan (lines 90-170, bytes 2-12, the cursor's cell white), a page of 18 tiles (pages of 18, - and + turn them) with the letters A-O, Q-S (SO0+14Bh maps scan codes to them; a letter after P counts one less). New maps are the two plain seas in a checkerboard. The check (ovl3_8146): from every sea cell beside a port or crown that is not at the map's edge, a search (a stack, through way_blocked and coast_pass) must reach the edge ("ALL PORTS MUST HAVE A SUPPLY PATH."), and every port and crown (74-77) needs a sea neighbour whose shore faces it ("ALL PORTS MUST BE NEXT TO WATER.").
- **The ships editor** (ovl2_7650): load_sm_tiles draws the whole map into BUF; the marker (5CC7 byte, 5CC9 line pair, 5CCB address) moves 1 byte or line pair an iteration (blit, delay(500), and delay([0D19] / 2) when [0D19] > 1500); - and + scroll 2 line pairs (with Shift, [081A], to the top or bottom); the view follows the marker. sqed_read_key reads [0804] and then sets it to [0802], so a key held down is read again every iteration: a key held past one look acts twice (Fleet's counts vanish at once if F is still held; Enter at the second step's "Press any key." goes straight on to "Continue to the next step?"; checked in DOSBox), and a cursor key let go before the editor looks does nothing. On a squadron (the marker on its column within two line pairs) a buzz and "Info Get Remove". Info (ovl2_8457): squadron_info's panel; the item mark (invert_block at 0C85h + item x 140h) on a ship or the squadron's line; N types the name (ovl2_8f6b: 14 characters, upper case, a blinking block every 150 looks at the keyboard; + or - on an empty name goes to the next or last item; a name another ship has: "WARNING, ANOTHER SHIP HAS THIS NAME."), a new ship takes the first free slot (a flagship unless the squadron has one); T, S, H, R; on the squadron's line F friend or foe, S supplies. A squadron left with no ships goes. Enter: both sides must have ships ("MUST HAVE BOTH FRIEND AND FOE SQUADS."). SO1's "BOTH SIDES MUST HAVE A FLAG SHIP." is never used.
